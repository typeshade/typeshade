**Rule 11.12.** An integer division, remainder and shift, and a float's conversion to an integer, must give WGSL's answer on GLSL ES 3.00 for every input WGSL settles: the GLSL writer spells each through a helper that settles the inputs GLSL ES 3.00 leaves undefined, and uses the bare operator only where the operands cannot reach one.

- Rationale: GLSL ES 3.00 gives a zero divisor, the least `int` over -1, a negative operand of `%`, a shift amount past the width and a float the integer cannot hold no result (its §5.9, §4.1.3 and §5.4.1), and a WebGL2 driver answers otherwise than WGSL on all but the shift (#382); the oracle follows WGSL (Rule 11.5), so without the helpers a program means one thing on WebGPU and on the CPU tier and another on WebGL2, a kernel function's WebGL2 tier included.
- Derives from: change `0027` in `changes/`; #382; [Arithmetic Expressions](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/wgsl/#arithmetic-expr), [Bit Expressions](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/wgsl/#bit-expr) and [Conversion Expressions](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/wgsl/#conversion-expr).
- Enforced by: `src/core/backends/glsl-int.ts`, which the GLSL writer's `intBinop` and `floatToInt` spellings call; pinned by `src/core/backends/glsl-int-answers.test.ts` on both halves; the compile gate for every example's GLSL; and the WebGL2 arm of `scripts/gpu-differential.ts`, which holds these operations to the oracle over generated programs and requires the corpus to reach the inputs GLSL leaves undefined.
